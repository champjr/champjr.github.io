If you like the idea of personal finance software but hate the idea of handing your money data to yet another mystery cloud, **Actual Budget** is a very compelling little rebel.

Actual is an open-source, local-first budgeting app built for people who want serious budgeting tools without the usual lock-in. You can run it locally, self-host it, or use one-click hosting if you want the convenience without babysitting a server. The big pitch is simple: your budget feels fast, private, and under your control.

## Quick tour

At its core, Actual is a personal finance app centered around envelope-style budgeting. Instead of vaguely hoping your checking account balance means everything is fine, you give every dollar a job. That makes it much easier to answer useful questions like “can I afford this?” instead of the less useful classic, “uh, probably?”

The app covers the basics you’d expect, like accounts, transactions, categories, reports, and recurring entries. But it also has a few standout traits that make it more interesting than just “spreadsheet, but shinier.”

First, it’s **local-first**. That means it’s designed so your data lives with you, not trapped behind a subscription-shaped curtain. Second, it supports **sync across devices** if you want it, so local-first does not have to mean lonely-single-laptop mode. Third, it has a reputation for being **fast and focused**, which matters a lot for software you’re supposed to open regularly instead of fearfully once a month.

There’s also support for importing data and a growing set of docs for people coming from other budgeting tools. So if you’ve been budget-app-curious but migration-phobic, Actual at least makes the first step feel less dramatic.

## Why it’s cool

A lot of finance tools treat privacy like a premium feature. Actual starts from the healthier assumption that your financial data is yours.

That open-source, self-hostable angle is already cool, but the nicer surprise is that Actual does not feel like a punishment for caring about ownership. It looks modern, the workflow is approachable, and it aims at real everyday budgeting instead of turning your finances into a weird productivity game.

I also like that it meets people where they are. You can download desktop apps, run it with Docker, or use lightweight hosted options if you want to skip the ops detour. That flexibility makes it feel welcoming to both tinkerers and normal humans, which is rarer than it should be.

## Who it’s for

Actual Budget is a great fit for:

- people who want more control over their financial data
- former spreadsheet budgeters who want structure without losing flexibility
- self-hosting fans who want an actually useful home-server app
- anyone curious about envelope budgeting and looking for a modern place to try it

If you want full investment tracking, tax planning, or enterprise-grade accounting, this is probably not your lane. But if you want a practical budgeting app that respects your autonomy, it’s a very good lane.

## Getting started

Smallest possible first step: go to the download or install page and choose **one** path, either the desktop app or the Docker setup.

If you want the easiest zero-commitment test, start with the desktop app and create a fresh budget. Add one account, one category group, and a couple of sample transactions. You’ll know pretty quickly whether the workflow clicks.

If you’re self-hosting already, the Docker install is the natural next move.
