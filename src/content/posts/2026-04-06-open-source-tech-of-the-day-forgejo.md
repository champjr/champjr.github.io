If GitHub-style collaboration is great but you’d rather host the whole thing yourself, Forgejo is worth a serious look. It’s a lightweight, open-source software forge for repositories, pull requests, issues, releases, and all the other infrastructure that turns “some code in a folder” into an actual collaborative project.

Forgejo grew out of the Gitea ecosystem and aims to provide a community-driven home for self-hosted development workflows. In practice, that means you get a familiar web interface for managing Git repos and team collaboration, but with more control over where it runs and how it’s governed. For a lot of teams, that’s a very appealing combination.

## Quick tour

At its core, Forgejo gives you the building blocks you expect from a modern code hosting platform:

- **Git repository hosting** with a clean web UI
- **Issues and pull requests** for collaboration and review
- **Actions/CI support** for automation
- **User, org, and permission management** for teams
- **Releases, packages, and project tools** that help round out the platform

One of the big selling points is that it’s comparatively lightweight. You don’t need to assemble a giant enterprise contraption just to host a few repos with decent workflows. Forgejo can serve a solo developer, a small open-source community, or a team that wants ownership over its tooling.

## Why it’s cool

Forgejo is cool because it speaks to a bigger open-source idea: not just open code, but open collaboration infrastructure. The tools we use to build software shape how we work together. Having a strong self-hosted option means more room for independence, experimentation, and community governance.

It’s also just practical. Maybe you want a forge for a private LAN project. Maybe you’re running an org that prefers self-hosting. Maybe you just enjoy knowing where your services live and who’s in charge. Forgejo meets that energy without feeling like a punishment.

And yes, there’s something satisfying about running your own forge. Tiny internet castle vibes, but productive.

## Who it’s for

Forgejo is a strong match for:

- Self-hosters and homelab folks
- Small teams that want Git hosting under their control
- Open-source communities that care about transparent governance
- Developers who want GitHub-like workflows without being locked into a hosted platform

If you’ve ever thought, “I want the forge, not the whole empire,” Forgejo is a compelling option.

## Getting started

Smallest possible first step: try the demo or launch Forgejo locally with Docker so you can click around before committing to anything bigger.

That low-friction trial is often enough to tell whether it fits your workflow.
